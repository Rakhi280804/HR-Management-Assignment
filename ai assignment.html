Assumptions I’m making

  • The brand is new, with no existing following, and sells packaged snacks (for example roasted makhana, protein bars, nut mixes) at ₹40-150 per pack.
  • ₹50,000 covers everything: samples, design, ads and local partnerships.
  • Sales will be mostly offline and via WhatsApp or Instagram orders at first, not a full e-commerce site.
  • Delivery is limited to Nagpur.

Customer segmentation

  1. Hostel and PG students (18-23): price-sensitive, snack often, influenced by friends and campus Instagram pages.
  2. Gym-going young professionals (22-30): care about protein and ingredients, will pay a premium.
  3. Office workers (24-30): want a better desk snack than biscuits and namkeen, buy in multi-packs.

Start with segments 1 and 2. They are the easiest to reach cheaply and the quickest to give feedback.

Positioning
“Everyday snacks that are actually good for you, at a price a student can afford.” Lead with one clear promise (for example, no palm oil, no refined sugar, simple ingredient list) rather than listing every health claim.

Channels and budget (₹50,000)

Weekly plan

  • Week 1 (prepare): finalise product range and pricing, set up Instagram and WhatsApp Business, shoot photos and short videos, line up 2-3 gym and college contacts.
  • Week 2 (soft launch): sampling at two campuses and two gyms, collect feedback with a short form, begin posting 4-5 times a week.
  • Week 3 (push): onboard micro-influencers, launch a first-order offer, start geo-targeted ads, place stock in 5-10 shops.
  • Week 4 (convert): double down on what worked, launch a referral offer, gather reviews and photos, plan month two.

Performance indicators

  • Samples given vs. trial-to-purchase conversion rate
  • First-time buyers (target: around 300-500 in 30 days)
  • Repeat purchase rate by day 30
  • Cost per acquisition by channel
  • Instagram follower growth and engagement
  • Number of retail points stocking the product

Market research still required

  • What competitors (local brands and national ones like Yoga Bar or Open Secret) charge, and where they sell in Nagpur
  • Which flavours and price points students will pay for
  • Whether gyms and canteens will stock a new brand, and on what margin
  • Sourcing, packaging and shelf-life costs, which decide your actual margins
  • Which colleges and gyms allow sampling, and any permissions needed
  • Food licensing requirements (FSSAI) before you sell

This section works well. It builds on Technique 3, since each stage can carry its own context, and the example shows the key habit: one stage at a time, with a checkpoint before moving on.

Why the good prompt works

  • Stages in a logical order: each one feeds the next (opportunity, then customers, then positioning, then marketing, then costs and risks).
  • “Start with Stage 1 only”: it stops the AI from rushing through all five stages with shallow, invented detail.
  • “Ask me for missing information”: it turns the AI into a collaborator that gathers facts instead of guessing them.

Small improvements to consider

  • Define what each stage should produce. For example, “Stage 1: a one-page problem statement and three opportunity hypotheses.”
  • Add a review gate. “Summarise your conclusions and wait for my approval before Stage 2.”
  • Carry decisions forward. “At each stage, restate the decisions we’ve confirmed so far.”
  • Explain why the bad prompt fails. “Start a business for me” gives the AI nothing to decompose, so it produces generic advice.

Reusable template

Project: [What we’re building or solving]
Stages: [3-6 numbered stages, each with its expected output]
Process rule: Work on one stage at a time. Ask for missing information first. Summarise and wait for approval before moving on.

What the AI’s first reply to the good prompt would look like
Stage 1 would cover the customer problem (for example, people want snacks that are healthy but find them expensive, bland or hard to trust) and a few opportunity hypotheses. Before going further, it would ask for the missing inputs:

  • Which city or region you’ll start in
  • Your budget and timeline
  • Your product ideas and ingredient sourcing
  • Any early evidence of demand (surveys, conversations, competitors you’ve studied)

Also, the guide jumps from Technique 3 to Technique 5, so Technique 4 may be missing from what you’ve pasted.
